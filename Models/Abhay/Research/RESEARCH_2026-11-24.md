# Fresh-split retrieval study, 24 November 2026

Model 6's first evaluation split was inspected repeatedly, so it cannot be reused as an untouched test. A new screening study used identity-disjoint splits at seeds 2031–2035, followed by predeclared check splits at seeds 2036–2040. Each episode held out whole near-duplicate groups as queries and ranked same-species gallery identities by their strongest image match. This is closed-set matching and does not measure Kaggle open-set MAP@5.

An exact-view or same-flank bonus usually reduced performance on screening splits and was dropped. A gallery hub penalty was more stable: for each gallery image, compute the mean cosine to its 30 nearest images of *different* gallery identities, then subtract 0.25 times that mean from its query similarity. The 0.25 weight was selected on screening splits; it was fixed before the five check splits.

| Check seed | Cosine MAP@5 | Hub-adjusted MAP@5 | Difference |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2036 | 0.8659 | 0.8674 | +0.0015 |
| 2037 | 0.8613 | 0.8690 | +0.0077 |
| 2038 | 0.8764 | 0.8741 | −0.0023 |
| 2039 | 0.8709 | 0.8779 | +0.0070 |
| 2040 | 0.8333 | 0.8456 | +0.0123 |

The simple mean of episode differences is about +0.0052 MAP@5. Four of five check episodes improved, one worsened. Episodes share source identities across seeds, so they are correlated; this is evidence of a small repeatable direction, not an independent five-fold confidence interval or a competition improvement. The gain is too small to justify presenting a new final model by itself.

The reproducible screening code is `view_study.py` in the working research copy. It currently runs seeds 2036–2040; changing that tuple to 2031–2035 reproduces screening. The next substantial retrieval design should be judged against both plain cosine and this fixed hub correction on predeclared splits.
