Animal ReID: where can matching improve?

25 November 2026. Reproducible analysis of supplied metadata, 2,152-dimensional MIEW-ID features and both extracted SIFT directories. Sources were read only.

How to interpret these numbers
  • Raw cosine retrieval is a diagnostic baseline, not Dalvin's full PCA/LDA + SIFT + LightGBM pipeline. Candidate recall here is for raw cosine, not Dalvin's shortlist.
  • Self-excluded retrieval removes only the query. Group-excluded retrieval removes its entire within-identity near-duplicate component (cosine ≥0.97). Identities without another group are excluded from the eligible average; use the matched-query comparison in summary.json to isolate duplicate effects.
  • Training photos rotate through query and gallery roles. Scores are descriptive, correlated and not an untouched validation result. Duplicate groups approximate encounters; they are not confirmed encounter IDs.
  • Open-set simulations use 25% held-out identities per species, seeds 2059–2061, and group-separated known queries. Thresholds are measured on each episode's known queries to describe overlap, not independently calibrated deployment thresholds. Episodes reuse photos.
  • SIFT verification samples up to 40 unique pairs per species/category, selected from each query's best available pair. Categories are not representative random pair populations. Mutual nearest matching plus a forward 0.85 ratio test and 15-pixel partial-affine RANSAC reproduce the preserved baseline's matching procedure. Seven inliers is a feature threshold, not a standalone identity decision.
  • Test identities are unknown. High similarity cannot establish that a test animal is known, nor estimate the test unknown fraction. Metadata coverage and descriptor shapes are checked; all SIFT descriptor values are not scanned for finiteness.

What this suggests we should improve

  1. Prioritize missing-side and missing-view coverage. Among left/right query photos, raw matching falls dramatically when the correct identity has no same-side gallery image. This remains difficult with PCA/LDA in the separate episode. An experiment should focus on same-view, same-side and opposite-side evidence separately, and make uncertainty depend on gallery coverage. Gallery size and animal difficulty are confounders; this association does not prove that adding a viewpoint feature will improve the complete model.
  2. Test candidate expansion on difficult queries. In the single PCA/LDA episode, correct-identity recall among 15 candidates is 97.34% for hyenas and 95.49% for leopards. A ranker cannot recover an identity outside its shortlist. Compare the current 15 candidates with an adaptive 30–50, or a union from raw and LDA retrieval, on a reserved split. These figures omit Dalvin's SIFT shortlist boost, so they do not measure his actual shortlist recall.
  3. Evaluate small galleries explicitly. Many identities have very few photos, whereas a few dominate the image count. Report both per-image and per-identity metrics, plus slices by usable gallery size. The same-view disadvantage persists within small/large gallery strata. Balance training episodes by identity and coverage as a hypothesis to test.
  4. Improve unknown-animal decisions with coverage-aware evidence. Across six raw-cosine open-set episodes, accepting about 95% of known queries also accepts 61–84% of unknown queries. A single similarity threshold is inadequate here. Compare the complete baseline's errors across species, viewpoints, gallery sizes and SIFT-support levels before changing its unknown detector.
  5. Keep SIFT's role conditional on viewpoint. Inspect the sampled verification table below before treating low inlier counts as evidence for a new animal. A different side or pose can prevent geometric alignment. SIFT sample sizes are small and deliberately selected; test any proposed scoring change in the full pipeline.
  6. Account for train/test composition. Hyenas represent 26.69% of training photos and 43.60% of test photos. Use species-specific reporting and calibration checks; do not infer test identity prevalence from this shift.

Suggested first experiment: preserve the full Dalvin baseline and compare an adaptive union shortlist against its current shortlist, concentrating analysis on missing-side and small-gallery queries. Measure correct-identity shortlist recall, known-query MAP@5, unknown-query MAP@5 and overall MAP@5 on a newly reserved evaluation split. The present EDA has already examined these data, so it must not be presented as an untouched final evaluation.

Scope: these are measured diagnostic weaknesses and proposed experiments. No improvement to the complete Dalvin pipeline or competition score is established.

Identity imbalance

204 of 345 leopard identities and 42 of 205 hyena identities have at most three photos. Equal weighting of eligible identities gives substantially lower raw MAP@5 than weighting each query photo equally. Singleton-only identities cannot enter this retrieval average. The feature matrix is finite and nonzero; metadata has missing viewpoints, which should be represented explicitly. Exactly two training rows share an identical feature vector, both labelled LEO0032; there are no exact feature duplicates across train and test.

SIFT availability limits

All 9,954 images have descriptor and coordinate files with consistent shapes. However, 96 have no keypoints and 155 have fewer than four, preventing the preserved baseline from attempting geometric verification for any pair involving those images. Missing evidence must not be treated as a verified non-match. Descriptor dimension is 128 throughout. Training metadata has 136 missing viewpoints and test metadata has 42.
